## Why this exists

Dolores’s March 30, 2026 correspondence describes an August counselor training and says the objectives should be set before use. The Provider Pilot Operations Studio preserves that instruction but stops at the operating-plan level. The Provider Activation Workbook makes the next local artifact executable: what the session should teach, what a reviewer should demonstrate, what synthetic failure modes should be rehearsed, and what governed evidence must come back before a site can claim completion.

The source does not specify a duration, facilitator, roster, approved curriculum, completed session, or competency standard. The workbook therefore labels its 100-minute run-of-show, eight objectives, four drills, and observation language as working design controls. It does not present them as a source-approved program.

## Working curriculum

Four modules mirror the Pilot Operations Studio:

1. hold the intended-use, unchanged-Findings, human-review, and prohibited-use boundary;
2. trace the source evidence, review the draft, correct or return it, and preserve only the reviewed artifact;
3. route critical screens, invoke stop and incident paths, and preserve named restart authority;
4. record structured corrections, denominators, incidents, and quarterly learning without turning workflow evidence into an outcome.

The working allocation is 20, 30, 30, and 20 minutes. That 100-minute design is locally proposed, not scheduled or accepted.

## Eight observable objectives

Before any authorized use, a reviewer should be able to:

- state the intended use and unchanged Findings authority;
- select the minimum-necessary audience format;
- trace narrative and hypothesis claims to visible source evidence;
- complete an explicit approve-or-return disposition;
- route a critical screen without treating generated prose as the response;
- recognize approval reopening, rescoring, and superseded artifacts;
- hold safely when generation, integration, delivery, or integrity evidence is unavailable;
- record structured learning without claiming accuracy, reliability, or patient benefit.

No local control records an objective as passed. That requires a qualified, authenticated facilitator and a governed observation record outside this sandbox.

## Four synthetic drills

The workbook uses three frozen synthetic PERL records and one disabled-connector condition:

1. `FF-TEST-2411-C` — ordinary evidence-linked review;
2. `FF-TEST-2407-A` — non-zero critical-screen hold and escalation;
3. `FF-TEST-2388-B` — approved-artifact change, reopened approval, and supersession;
4. `synthetic-disabled-connector` — no-write delivery hold, retry/dead-letter evidence, and external authority.

Exactly two drills are critical. A bypassed critical hold, invented result, unauthorized disclosure, or unsupported approval stops completion; no average score can erase it.

## Evidence before completion

Ten returns remain external:

1. accepted objectives and prohibited-use boundary;
2. named qualified facilitator and backup;
3. governed counselor roster;
4. site-owned license and role verification;
5. scheduled, accessible session record and approved materials;
6. governed attendance evidence;
7. facilitator-observed evidence for all eight objectives;
8. successful observation of both critical drills;
9. accessibility and accommodation acceptance;
10. named support, stop, incident, escalation, and restart path.

Attendance alone cannot become completion. A complete workbook cannot become site activation. The seven independent pilot-admission gates and a separate named-site authorization still control use.

Endpoints:

- `GET /api/governance/provider-activation`

## Claim boundary

The workbook receives no names, licenses, attendance, signatures, records, Findings content, files, credentials, or PHI. It does not conduct training, issue credit, replace site procedure or supervision, accept an objective, verify competency, activate a site, or authorize clinical use. Those decisions remain with named site, clinical, legal, security/privacy, accessibility, product, technical, and pilot authorities.
